How it works for you

Your policy and your money. GetThere runs the rest.

Issue the policy

The Flight Delay Pass is your policy, under your licence. You're named as its issuer on the offer, the checkout, the terms and every email.

YOU

Collect every fee

Travellers pay into your own payment account, through your provider's form embedded on the checkout page.

YOU

Fund every rescue card

Rescue cards are issued on your own card account, and only what the traveller spends on a new flight leaves it.

GETTHERE

Flight by flight, from each flight's own track record, weather and airport news, within your limits.

Offer pages, quote emails and checkout in each partner's colours. Reading each customer's booking email, or the booking a travel site sends.

GETTHERE

Who holds which risk

Three buckets of risk: what GetThere captures, what you control, what you can leave to your partner

Every quote comes from the same pricing model. Some risks it prices and screens for you on every partner. Some limits are yours alone. A few choices are left to each partner, and the model prices each of them in, so a partner's choice moves the traveller's price, not your margin.

GETTHERE CAPTURES

Priced and screened on every pass

  1. How often the flight is late A year of that flight's own departures, by month, route and airport.
  2. How much of the rescue card gets spent Most of a small rescue card is spent and less of a large one, so each plan size is priced on its own.
  3. How long the delay must be A shorter trigger is priced higher.
  4. Weather, strikes and air traffic control Affected airports stop selling until it passes.
  5. Inflated fares A fare far above the market gets no quote, and the rescue card follows what a new seat typically costs.
  6. Miles and points Only bookings paid in full with money are offered a pass.
  7. Rescue card misuse One purchase, within 24 hours, at airlines and travel agencies only.
YOU CONTROL

Your limits, a ceiling for every partner

  1. Exposure per flight The most your passes may hold in rescue card limits on one flight and date.
  2. Exposure per airport The same for one airport on one day.
  3. Repeat claimers How many rescue cards a traveller may have in a year.
  4. Buying for others How many passes a year for trips the buyer isn't on.
  5. Rate floor and sales close Raise the lowest rate and close sales earlier. Never below GetThere's own.
  6. Highest risk sold The chance of disruption above which a booking is declined.
  7. Booking size and rescue card size The smallest and largest booking, and the rescue card's ceiling against the fare paid.
YOUR PARTNER DECIDES

Priced in automatically

  1. The delay trigger 3 hours by default; a premium card or a travel site's premium fare might offer 2. The price rises to match.
  2. Plan sizes Rescue cards from 1 to 3 times the seat price. Bigger plans cost more.
  3. Tighter limits A partner can tighten any of your defaults for its own customers, never loosen them.
  4. Who sees the offer Which of a Card Issuer's cards and charges get the nudge, or which of a travel site's bookings see the offer.
  5. A purchase check For a Card Issuer, an optional yes or no that the booking was paid on its card, for a stronger fraud check.
  6. Look and channel Its colours, logo, app and emails.
Fee = chance of disruption × expected spend on the rescue card × rescue card limit ÷ (1 − gross margin). GetThere sets the first two from its model. The rescue card limit comes from the partner's plans within your ceiling. The margin is yours.

Exposure

One cancelled flight or one closed airport can only cost what you allow

A cancellation pays every pass on the flight at once, and a typhoon pays every pass through the airport. So you cap the total rescue card limits on both, in US dollars, across every partner you underwrite.

Per flight

The most your active passes may hold in rescue card limits on one flight on one date.

Per airport, per day

The most on every flight leaving or arriving at one airport on one day. A pass counts once per airport.

Checked to the last seat

At quote, at checkout, and once more under a lock as the pass is created, so two buyers can't both take the last of the room.

These limits are yours alone. No partner can raise them, and other underwriters' passes never count against yours.

Claims

No claim forms, no adjusters, no cash

The trigger is agreed up front: a delay past the partner's trigger, 3 hours by default, or a cancellation. Flight data opens the claim and the rescue card goes out within minutes.

Spent on flights only

The rescue card is a single-use virtual card that works only at airlines and travel agencies. It's never paid out as cash.

One purchase in 24 hours

The traveller books the rest of the trip in one go, within 24 hours. Every charge passes the rescue card's spending controls first.

You pay what's spent

The rescue card limit is a ceiling, not a transfer. If the new flight costs less, the rest never leaves your account.

If the airline cancels a flight before GetThere starts watching it, the fee is refunded and no rescue card goes out, as the terms say.

Money and data

GetThere never holds your money or payment card data

Your accounts, your keys

Real passes use only your own payment gateway and rescue card issuing accounts, with your keys stored encrypted. Every payment, refund and rescue card goes back to the account it came from.

No payment card data

Payment card details are typed into your payment provider's form, embedded on the page. GetThere never sees them.

Your wording, kept as sold

Terms are built from your settings. Each version is stored when it goes live and never changed, and each pass records the version it was sold under.

Watched every few minutes

The travel-day sweep checks every active flight, and a daily reconciliation flags any delay without a claim, or any claim without a rescue card, to the operations team.

Every dollar tracked digitally, with no manual invoicing. Every Flight Delay Pass payment, every refund of a pass voided in the cooling-off period, and every rescue card with each purchase made on it is recorded as it happens, so what is left on each rescue card is known to the cent. The monthly figures and each partner's share come from those records, not from invoices built by hand.
Sales tax, set by you for each country. You set the sales tax you owe in each country you sell in, by name and rate (GST 9% in Singapore, say). Checkout adds it on top of the fee for the buyer's country of residence and shows it before they pay. Every payment records the country, rate and amount, and your monthly report totals the tax collected in each country for your filing.
