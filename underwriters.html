Who holds which risk

Three buckets of risk

GETTHERE CAPTURES

Each pass is priced from the flight's own delay record, the trigger and the plan size, and screened for weather, strikes, inflated fares, miles bookings and rescue card misuse. A travel site's rescue cards work only at its own merchant IDs, and the site checks every rebook link with us.

YOU CONTROL

Your limits apply to every partner: exposure per flight and airport, repeat claimers, the rate floor, the highest risk sold, booking and rescue card size, and how close to departure sales stop, set per kind of partner.

YOUR PARTNER DECIDES

The trigger, plan sizes, who sees the offer and its look. The trigger and plan size are priced in, so they move the traveller's price, not your margin. A partner can only tighten your limits. A travel site or platform also keeps the rebooking on its own site, which the price doesn't yet reflect.

Fee = chance of disruption × expected spend on the rescue card × rescue card limit ÷ (1 − gross margin). GetThere sets the first two from its model. The rescue card limit comes from the partner's plans within your ceiling. The margin is yours.

Exposure

One cancelled flight or one closed airport can only cost what you allow

A cancellation pays every pass on the flight at once, and a typhoon pays every pass through the airport. So you cap the total rescue card limits on both, in US dollars, across every partner you underwrite.

Per flight

The most your active passes may hold in rescue card limits on one flight on one date.

Per airport, per day

The most on every flight leaving or arriving at one airport on one day. A pass counts once per airport.

Checked to the last seat

At quote, at checkout, and once more under a lock as the pass is created, so two buyers can't both take the last of the room.

These limits are yours alone. No partner can raise them, and other underwriters' passes never count against yours.

Claims

No claim forms, no adjusters, no cash

The trigger is agreed up front: a delay past the partner's trigger, 3 hours by default, or a cancellation. Flight data opens the claim and the rescue card goes out within minutes.

Spent on flights only

The rescue card is a single-use virtual card that works only at airlines and travel agencies. It's never paid out as cash.

One purchase in 24 hours

The traveller books the rest of the trip in one go, within 24 hours. Every charge passes the rescue card's spending controls first.

You pay what's spent

The rescue card limit is a ceiling, not a transfer. If the new flight costs less, the rest never leaves your account.

If the airline cancels a flight before GetThere starts watching it, the fee is refunded and no rescue card goes out, as the terms say.

Rolling out to partners

Distribute the policy through Card Issuers, Loyalty Programs, Travel Sites and Travel Platforms

GetThere builds and runs the offer pages, quote emails, checkout, terms and rescue cards for every partner.

Card Issuers send one push notification after an airline charge on their card. Three more pushes and a yes-or-no purchase check are optional. Card Issuer page
Loyalty Programs send one push notification after a member's flight purchase they track, from a linked card or a click-through. Members can earn miles on the pass, funded by the program from its own share, so your margin is untouched. Loyalty Program page
Travel Sites show the offer on their booking confirmation page, and the customer's checkout opens already filled in. The same three pushes are optional, and their rescue cards work on their own site only. Travel Site page
Travel Platforms offer the pass to the company employees who book on them. They send each booking's flights as it's made, so the price is ready at once, and rescue cards rebook through their own booking tool. Travel Platform page

Every integration is in the API reference.

Money and data

All the money in your own accounts, and GetThere never sees card data

Your own gateway and card issuer

You bring the vendors' APIs, GetThere brings the integrations. Every pass fee and top-up is paid into your own gateway account, refunds and disputes are handled there, and rescue cards are issued on your own card issuer account. GetThere never holds your money. Your keys are stored encrypted. See the vendors we support and how to connect them.

No payment card data

Payment card details are typed into your gateway's own payment page. GetThere never sees them.

Your wording, kept as sold

Terms are built from your settings. Each version is stored when it goes live and never changed, and each pass records the version it was sold under.

Watched every few minutes

The travel-day sweep checks every active flight, and a daily reconciliation flags any delay without a claim, or any claim without a rescue card, to the operations team.

Every dollar tracked digitally, with no manual invoicing. Every Flight Delay Pass payment, every refund of a pass voided in the cooling-off period, and every rescue card with each purchase made on it is recorded as it happens, so what is left on each rescue card is known to the cent. The monthly figures and each partner's share come from those records, not from invoices built by hand.
Sales tax, set by you for each country. You set the sales tax you owe in each country you sell in, by name and rate (GST 9% in Singapore, say). Checkout adds it on top of the fee for the buyer's country of residence and shows it before they pay. Every payment records the country, rate and amount, and your monthly report totals the tax collected in each country for your filing.
