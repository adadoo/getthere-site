Who holds which risk

Three buckets of risk: what GetThere captures, what you control, what you can leave to your partner

Every quote comes from the same pricing model. Some risks it prices and screens for you on every partner. Some limits are yours alone. A few choices are left to each partner, and the model prices each of them in, so a partner's choice moves the traveller's price, not your margin.

GETTHERE CAPTURES

Priced and screened on every pass

  1. How often the flight is late A year of that flight's own departures, by month, route and airport. A flight with no history starts at 2%, and none goes below 1%.
  2. How much of the card gets spent Most of a small card is spent and less of a large one, so each plan size is priced on its own.
  3. How long the delay must be A shorter trigger is priced higher: a 2-hour trigger at 1.6 times the 3-hour rate.
  4. Weather, strikes and air traffic control Affected airports stop selling until it passes.
  5. Inflated fares A fare far above the market gets no quote, and the card follows what a new seat typically costs.
  6. Miles and points Only bookings paid in full with money are offered a pass.
  7. Card misuse One purchase, within 24 hours, at airlines and travel agencies only.
YOU CONTROL

Your limits, a ceiling for every partner

  1. Exposure per flight The most your passes may hold in card limits on one flight and date.
  2. Exposure per airport The same for one airport on one day.
  3. Repeat claimers How many rescue cards a traveller may have in a year.
  4. Buying for others How many passes a year for trips the buyer isn't on.
  5. Rate floor and sales close Raise the lowest rate and close sales earlier. Never below GetThere's own.
  6. Highest risk sold The chance of disruption above which a booking is declined.
  7. Booking size and card size The smallest and largest booking, and the card's ceiling against the fare paid.
  8. Margin per partner Your gross margin on each partner's passes, 60% unless you agree otherwise.
YOUR PARTNER DECIDES

Priced in automatically

  1. The delay trigger 3 hours by default; a premium card might offer 2. The price rises to match.
  2. Plan sizes Cards from 1 to 3 times the seat price. Bigger plans cost more.
  3. Tighter limits A partner can tighten any of your defaults for its own customers, never loosen them.
  4. Who sees the offer Which cards, which charges and which customers get the nudge.
  5. A purchase check An optional yes or no that the booking was paid on its card, for a stronger fraud check.
  6. Look and channel Its colours, logo, app and emails.
Fee = chance of disruption × expected spend on the rescue card × card limit ÷ (1 − gross margin). GetThere sets the first two from its model. The card limit comes from the partner's plans within your ceiling. The margin is yours.

Exposure

One cancelled flight or one closed airport can only cost what you allow

A cancellation pays every pass on the flight at once, and a typhoon pays every pass through the airport. So you cap the total card limits on both, in US dollars, across every partner you underwrite.

Per flight

The most your active passes may hold in card limits on one flight on one date.

Per airport, per day

The most on every flight leaving or arriving at one airport on one day. A pass counts once per airport.

Checked to the last seat

At quote, at checkout, and once more under a lock as the pass is created, so two buyers can't both take the last of the room.

These limits are yours alone. No partner can raise them, and other underwriters' passes never count against yours.

Claims

No claim forms, no adjusters, no cash

The trigger is agreed up front: a delay past the partner's trigger, 3 hours by default, or a cancellation. Flight data opens the claim and the rescue card goes out within minutes.

Spent on flights only

A single-use virtual card that works only at airlines and travel agencies. It's never paid out as cash.

One purchase in 24 hours

The traveller books the rest of the trip in one go, within 24 hours. Every charge passes the card's spending controls first.

You pay what's spent

The card limit is a ceiling, not a transfer. If the new flight costs less, the rest never leaves your account.

If the airline cancels a flight before GetThere starts watching it, the fee is refunded and no card goes out, as the terms say.

Money and data

GetThere never holds your money or card data

Your accounts, your keys

Real passes use only your own payment and card issuing accounts, with your keys stored encrypted. Every payment, refund and card goes back to the account it came from.

No card data

Card details are typed into your payment provider's form, embedded on the page. GetThere never sees them.

Your wording, kept as sold

Terms are built from your settings. Each version is stored when it goes live and never changed, and each pass records the version it was sold under.

Watched every few minutes

The travel-day sweep checks every active flight, and a daily reconciliation flags any delay without a claim, or any claim without a card, to the operations team.
