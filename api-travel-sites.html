For online travel agencies

API guide for online travel agencies

Version 1 · Draft · Written for the developers who build it. Keys, error format, retries, test mode and webhook signatures are on the basics page. Card issuer? See the card issuer guide.

What you're building. Your customer books a flight with you. You offer them the Flight Delay Pass for that booking. If a flight on it is delayed past the trigger (usually 3 hours) or cancelled, GetThere sends the customer a pre-loaded rescue card within minutes. It works for one purchase, on your site only, so they rebook with you. The pass is a policy the Underwriter issues. GetThere prices it, tracks the flights, issues the rescue card and handles every message to the customer.

1. Choose your path

Two decisions shape everything you build. Make them first. You can start with one path and move to another later on the same keys.

Decision 1: when do you offer the pass?

After booking. The flight is booked and paid for. On your confirmation page (or in your confirmation email) you offer the pass, and the customer pays for it on GetThere's checkout, in your colours. You take no payment and settle nothing. This is the quickest to build.

In your checkout (in-line). The pass is an add-on in your own flight checkout, next to seats and bags. The customer pays you once for both. When the airline confirms the booking you tell us, and we issue the pass at once. You settle with GetThere monthly. More customers buy when the offer comes before payment, but you build more. We switch your partner to in-line checkout for you.

Decision 2: whose design shows the plans?

Our widget. You put the merchandising_url from the quote in an iframe. It shows the plans in your colours, font and layout, which we set with you in the GetThere admin. It records what the customer saw and picked for you, and keeps the wording in line with the terms.

Your own design. You draw the plans yourself from the quote's offers. It looks native, but you must show the required wording (see the rules) and send us the funnel events the widget would have sent.

2a. After booking, with our widget

  1. The airline confirms the booking. From your server, call POST /partner/v1/quotes with the booking reference, flights, fare, travellers and the booker's email. Add placement: "post_booking".
  2. If status is quoted, put merchandising_url in an iframe on your confirmation page, or link to it from your confirmation email. Any other status: show nothing.
  3. The customer picks a plan. The widget opens GetThere's checkout, filled in with what you sent, in the full window. They confirm their details and WhatsApp number, tick the boxes and pay.
  4. Optional: set a webhook address to hear about the pass, its rescue card and any bounce.

That's the whole integration: one call and one iframe.

2b. After booking, with your own design

  1. Call POST /partner/v1/quotes as in 2a.
  2. Draw the plans from offers, following the rules for your own design. Send shown to POST /events when they're on screen.
  3. The customer picks one: send selected, then call POST /partner/v1/checkouts with its offer_id, and send the customer to the checkout_url that comes back.

2c. In your checkout, with our widget

  1. The customer's cart has flights in it (on the cart or checkout page). Call POST /partner/v1/quotes with no booking_reference, your cart or session id as session_id, and placement. Quote again whenever the flights, travellers or fare change: the earlier offers are withdrawn.
  2. Frame merchandising_url where your add-ons are. When the customer picks a plan, the widget sends your page a getthere:plan message with the offer_id and fee. Add it to your cart at that fee.
  3. At payment, show the two consent boxes (wording) next to the add-on and record the time the customer paid (accepted_at). Collect the buyer's name, date of birth and address if you don't already.
  4. The airline confirms the booking. Call POST /partner/v1/policies with the offer_id, each flight's booking reference, your order id, the payment and the buyer. You get the pass back straight away, and we email it to the customer.
  5. If the customer removes the pass from the cart, send removed to POST /events.
  6. Later changes: tell us with /changes or /cancel.
  7. Each month, pay the statement we send you.

2d. In your checkout, with your own design

As 2c, but you draw the plans from offers and must send every funnel event yourself: shown when the plans are on screen, selected when one is added, removed when it's taken out, and abandoned when the cart is given up. Here's why that matters. Follow the rules for your own design. If your cart can't hold our offer_id, call GET /quotes?session_id= at payment to get it back.

Rules for your own design

  • Show each plan's label, fee with its currency, and rescue_card_limit ("Pre-loaded rescue card with SGD 984.96").
  • Show the fee as it comes: it always includes sales tax (0% where none is added). Say "incl." with its tax_label (like "incl. GST 9%"), or "incl. tax" when tax_label is null.
  • Say what triggers the rescue card, using trigger_minutes ("delayed 3+ hours or cancelled"), and that it works for one purchase, for new flights, on your site.
  • Link to terms_url.
  • Put the featured plan first, or mark it, as the widget does. We record that you were told which plan was featured.
  • Don't describe the pass in your own words beyond this. The words used to sell it are regulated, and we agree them with the Underwriter.

4. The widget: merchandising_url

Paths 2a and 2c · after a quoted response

<iframe src="https://<you>.getthere.now/quote/q4LT…" title="Flight Delay Pass"
        style="width:100%;max-width:560px;border:0;height:520px" loading="lazy"></iframe>

It shows the customer's flights, each plan's fee and rescue card limit, and a button per plan, in the look we set with you. It needs no key. Once the booking has a pass, it shows the pass instead.

  • After booking, a plan's button opens GetThere's checkout in the full window.
  • In your checkout, a plan's button tells your page instead:
window.addEventListener("message", (e) => {
  if (e.origin !== "https://<you>.getthere.now") return;      // only our widget
  if (e.data?.type !== "getthere:plan") return;
  const {offer_id, plan, fee, currency} = e.data;            // e.g. "de01b698-…", "plus", 48.32, "SGD"
  addToCart({sku: "flight-delay-pass", offer_id, plan, price: fee, currency});
});

9. Funnel events: POST /partner/v1/events

This is not optional if you draw the plans yourself. Funnel events are how you, we and the Underwriter learn what sells the pass on your site, and how we keep the price right for your customers:
  • Your prices and plans. When we and the Underwriter review your prices and which plan to feature, we look at how often each plan is picked once it's shown. Without shown and selected, that review rests on other sites' customers, not yours.
  • Your conversion numbers. Your funnel (quoted, shown, selected, removed, pass created, WhatsApp added, rescue card issued and used) is only as good as the events behind it. With no shown it can't tell a pass nobody saw from one nobody wanted.
  • Your A/B tests. placement and ab_variant results come from these events.
  • Proof of what the customer saw. If a customer later says they never chose the pass, shown, selected and removed are the record of what happened.
Our widget sends shown and selected for you. Everyone sends removed and abandoned.

10. Webhooks

Set a test and a live address with us, and we'll tell your systems about every pass: issued, changed, ended, an email that bounced, WhatsApp added, a rescue card issued and used. The format, retries, every event type and how to check the signature are on the basics page. The three to handle first:

  • pass.issued: your record of the sale.
  • email.bounced: the customer won't get their pass or rescue card by email. Contact them and correct it.
  • rescue_card.used: they've rebooked with you, using the rescue card.

11. Your merchant IDs

A rescue card from your site works on your site only, so the customer rebooks with you. We hold the card to that with the merchant IDs your card payments carry: the IDs your payment provider (acquirer) gives each of your merchant accounts. Once we have them, a charge is approved only if it carries one of them, whatever name the merchant trades under. We can't switch you live without them. Send us every merchant ID your flight bookings are charged under (each country, each acquirer). If you add an acquirer later, send us its IDs before it takes payments.
