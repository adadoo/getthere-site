For travel sites

API guide for travel sites

Version 1 · Draft · Written for the developers who build it. Keys, error format, retries, test mode and webhook signatures are on the basics page. Card issuer? See the card issuer guide.

What you're building. Your customer books a flight with you. You offer them the Flight Delay Pass for that booking. If a flight on it is delayed past the trigger (usually 3 hours) or cancelled, GetThere sends the customer a pre-loaded rescue card within minutes. It works for one purchase, on your site only, so they rebook with you. The pass is a policy the Underwriter issues. GetThere prices it, tracks the flights, issues the rescue card and handles every message to the customer.

1. Choose your path

Every quote you ask for says where you'll show it, in its placement. The placement decides how the pass is sold, so it's the first thing to choose. There's no setting on our side to switch: you can use one placement, two or all three, on the same keys, and add one later.

Where do you show the plans?

booking-page: on your confirmation page. The flight is booked and paid for. You show the plans on the page that confirms it. When the customer chooses a plan, GetThere's checkout opens, in your colours, and they pay there. You take no payment and settle nothing. This is the quickest to build.

in-line: in your own checkout, before payment. The pass is an add-on in your flight checkout, next to seats and bags. You quote the cart, the customer pays you once for both, and when the airline confirms the booking you create the pass with POST /policies. You settle with GetThere monthly. More customers buy when the offer comes before payment, but you build more.

booking-email: in your own booking confirmation email. The plans go in the email you already send, like an ad. A plan's link always opens GetThere's checkout, as on the booking page. Email apps don't run frames or scripts, so here you link to the plans rather than embed them (2c).

Whose design shows the plans?

Our widget. You put the quote's merchandising_url in an iframe, or link to it. It shows the plans in your colours and font. Each placement has its own widget design, which you set with us in the GetThere admin: layout, background, button style, width, and whether to show the logo, intro, flight and badges. All three start out identical. The widget records what the customer saw and picked for you, and keeps the wording in line with the terms.

Your own design. You draw the plans yourself from the quote's offers. It looks native, but you must show the required wording (see the rules) and send us the funnel events the widget would have sent.

2a. On your booking page

  1. Required: the customer picks flights. Send them to POST /partner/v1/flights, so the quote is priced on each flight's own record.
  2. The airline confirms the booking. From your server, call POST /partner/v1/quotes with placement: "booking-page", the booking reference, flights, fare, travellers, and the booker's email and residence_country.
  3. If status is quoted, put merchandising_url in an iframe on your confirmation page. Any other status: show nothing.
  4. The customer chooses a plan. The widget opens GetThere's checkout, filled in with what you sent, in the full window. They confirm their details and WhatsApp number, tick the boxes and pay. The price shown includes the tax for the country they live in.
  5. Optional: set a webhook address to hear about the pass, its rescue card and any bounce.

That's the whole integration: one call and one iframe.

With your own design: draw the plans from offers, following the rules for your own design, and send shown to POST /events when they're on screen. When the customer chooses one, send selected, call POST /partner/v1/checkouts with its offer_id, and send the customer to the checkout_url that comes back.

2b. In-line, in your own checkout

  1. Required: the customer picks flights. Send them to POST /partner/v1/flights, so the quote is priced on each flight's own record.
  2. The customer's cart has flights in it. Call POST /partner/v1/quotes with placement: "in-line", your cart or session id as session_id, the flights, fare, travellers, and the booker's email and residence_country. There's no booking reference yet: if you send one, it's ignored. Quote again whenever the flights, travellers or fare change: the session's earlier offers are withdrawn.
  3. Frame merchandising_url where your add-ons are. When the customer chooses a plan, the widget sends your page a getthere:plan message with the offer_id and fee. Add it to your cart at that fee.
  4. At payment, show the two consent boxes (wording) next to the add-on and record the time the customer paid (accepted_at). Collect the buyer's name, date of birth and address if you don't already.
  5. The airline confirms the booking. Call POST /partner/v1/policies with the offer_id, each flight's booking reference, your order id, the payment and the buyer. You get the pass back straight away, and we email it to the customer.
  6. If the customer removes the pass from the cart, send removed to POST /events.
  7. Later changes: tell us with /changes or /cancel.
  8. Each month, pay the statement we send you.

With your own design: draw the plans from offers and send every funnel event yourself: shown when the plans are on screen, selected when one is added, removed when it's taken out, and abandoned when the cart is given up. Here's why that matters. Follow the rules for your own design. If your cart can't hold our offer_id, call GET /quotes?session_id= at payment to get it back.

2c. In your booking email

  1. Required: the customer picks flights. Send them to POST /partner/v1/flights, so the quote is priced on each flight's own record.
  2. The airline confirms the booking. Call POST /partner/v1/quotes as in 2a, with placement: "booking-email".
  3. If status is quoted, add the plans to your confirmation email. Any other status: leave them out.
  4. Link, don't embed. Email apps don't run iframes or scripts. Either add a button that links to merchandising_url, which opens the plans in a browser in your booking-email widget design, or draw the plans yourself and link each one to its own checkout: call POST /partner/v1/checkouts once per offer when you build the email, and use each checkout_url.
  5. The customer chooses a plan. Either way, it opens GetThere's checkout, and they pay there.

Send the email soon after you quote. Offers can be bought until their expires_at (12 hours by default, and never later than 48 hours before the first flight). After that the page says the prices have expired. When you link merchandising_url, the page records shown and selected as the customer opens it and chooses. When you draw the plans yourself in an email, you can't tell when they're seen, so no funnel events are expected.

Rules for your own design

  • Show each plan's label, fee with its currency, and rescue_card_limit ("Pre-loaded rescue card with SGD 984.96").
  • Show the fee as it comes, with "incl. tax", as the widget does: it always includes the tax for the residence_country you sent (0% where none is added). No breakdown is needed; fee_before_tax and tax are for your accounts.
  • Say what triggers the rescue card, using trigger_minutes ("delayed 3+ hours or cancelled"), and that it works for one purchase, for new flights, on your site.
  • Link to terms_url.
  • Put the featured plan first, or mark it, as the widget does. We record which plan was featured on each quote.
  • Don't describe the pass in your own words beyond this. The words used to sell it are regulated, and we agree them with the Underwriter.

3. POST /partner/v1/quotes

Every path · booking-page and booking-email: once the airline confirms · in-line: whenever the cart's flights, travellers or fare change

Prices the booking or cart. No email goes to the customer. A flight we've never priced before adds at most a second while we look up its history; it's then priced on its route, airport and airline until its own history is in.

Required, so the price is ready at once: as soon as the customer picks flights, from your server or your page's backend, send the flight numbers to POST /partner/v1/flights. It answers 202 at once, prices nothing and counts as no quote; it starts loading the history of any flight we've never priced, so by the time you quote, it's in. Up to 8 flights a call; calling it again for the same flights is harmless.

Quoting again. The same booking (or the same cart) quoted again returns the same open offers, and they take the placement, placement_detail and tags of the latest call. So if you show one booking on your confirmation page and in your email, the second quote's placement is the one your funnel counts and the one whose widget design merchandising_url shows. The one exception: a flight first priced before its own history was in is priced again on its own record once it is, so that call returns new offers. The earlier ones can still be bought until they expire, except in a cart, where a new quote replaces them as usual.

4. The widget: merchandising_url

Our widget, any placement · after a quoted response

<iframe src="https://<you>.getthere.now/quote/q4LT…" title="Flight Delay Pass"
        style="width:100%;max-width:560px;border:0;height:520px" loading="lazy"></iframe>

It shows the customer's flights, each plan's fee "incl. tax" and rescue card limit, and a Choose button per plan, in the design set for the quote's placement. Design changes you make in the admin show at once, on quotes already made too. It needs no key. Once the booking has a pass, it shows the pass instead. If the offers can no longer be bought, it says so in place of the plans.

  • booking-page and booking-email: a plan's button opens GetThere's checkout in the full window (it breaks out of your frame).
  • in-line: a plan's button reads "Added" and tells your page instead, with postMessage. Choosing another plan replaces it:
window.addEventListener("message", (e) => {
  if (e.origin !== "https://<you>.getthere.now") return;      // only our widget
  if (e.data?.type !== "getthere:plan") return;
  const {offer_id, plan, fee, currency} = e.data;            // e.g. "de01b698-…", "plus", 48.32, "SGD"
  addToCart({sku: "flight-delay-pass", offer_id, plan, price: fee, currency});
});

The same as {"type": "cancelled"}, for a booking that's been cancelled. Within the pass's cooling-off period it returns "status": "cancelled" and "pass_status": "refunded", and pass.ended carries reason: "refunded". After it, the pass ends with no refund: it returns "pass_status": "voided", and pass.ended carries reason: "cancelled" and refund: "0". When a customer disputes a payment taken on GetThere's checkout with their bank (a chargeback), an active pass ends: we send pass.ended with reason: "disputed" and refund: "0", and the customer is told. A pass that has already issued its rescue card keeps it, and GetThere contests the dispute.

9. Funnel events: POST /partner/v1/events

This is not optional if you draw the plans yourself on a page. Funnel events are how you, we and the Underwriter learn what sells the pass on your site, and how we keep the price right for your customers:
  • Your prices and plans. When we and the Underwriter review your prices and which plan to feature, we look at how often each plan is picked once it's shown. Without shown and selected, that review rests on other sites' customers, not yours.
  • Your conversion numbers. Your funnel (quoted, shown, selected, removed, pass created, WhatsApp added, rescue card issued and used, abandoned) is only as good as the events behind it. With no shown it can't tell a pass nobody saw from one nobody wanted.
  • Your A/B tests. The funnel splits by placement, placement_detail, ab_variant and the plan featured, and those splits come from these events.
  • Proof of what the customer saw. If a customer later says they never chose the pass, shown, selected and removed are the record of what happened.
Our widget sends shown and selected for you. In-line, everyone sends removed and abandoned.

10. Your forwarding address

Every partner has a forwarding address, quote@<you>.getthere.now. A customer who forwards a booking confirmation email to it gets the plans back by email within minutes, and buys on GetThere's checkout. It's a way in for customers you haven't quoted through the API: someone who booked before you integrated, or who found the offer elsewhere. Nothing to build: you only tell us where your booking emails come from.

  • Only your own booking emails. Your address reads only booking confirmations sent from your own domains, set as forwarding.booking_domains in the GetThere admin, like ["example-travel.com"]. A subdomain of one counts too. With none set, it accepts nothing.
  • Anything else is refused. An airline's email, or another site's, gets a reply saying we can only quote bookings made on your site and asking for your confirmation email instead. We record it as not_from_this_site.
  • Prices in the email are before tax. We don't know where a forwarding customer lives, so the email shows each plan before tax, and GetThere's checkout adds the tax for the country they choose there.
  • Test first. Until you go live, your address makes test passes. After that, test@<you>.getthere.now still does.

11. Webhooks

Set a test and a live address with us, and we'll tell your systems about every pass: issued, changed, ended, an email that bounced, WhatsApp added, a rescue card issued and used. That includes every placement, and passes from your forwarding address too. The format, retries, every event type and how to check the signature are on the basics page. The three to handle first:

  • pass.issued: your record of the sale.
  • email.bounced: the customer won't get their pass or rescue card by email. Contact them and correct it.
  • rescue_card.used: they've rebooked with you, using the rescue card.

12. Your merchant IDs

A rescue card from your site works on your site only, so the customer rebooks with you. We hold the card to that with the merchant IDs your card payments carry: the IDs your payment provider (acquirer) gives each of your merchant accounts. Once we have them, a charge is approved only if it carries one of them, whatever name the merchant trades under. We can't switch you live without them. Send us every merchant ID your flight bookings are charged under (each country, each acquirer). If you add an acquirer later, send us its IDs before it takes payments.

13. Your rebook page

When a flight is disrupted, the rescue card email and the rescue card page give the customer a Rebook on <your site> button. It's a GetThere link with its own random token, https://<you>.getthere.now/rebook/<token>, like every link we send. Opening it sends the customer straight on to your rebook page, with their booking and their trip from the disrupted flight onwards filled in. They pay there with the rescue card, which works on your site only (section 12).

This page is yours to build, and we can't switch you live without it. Give us its address as a template, for example:

https://www.yoursite.com/trips/rebook?booking={booking_id}&flights={flights}&date={date}

We fill in each placeholder, URL-encoded. It must include {booking_id} or {pnr}.

Test it on your staging site. Give us a second address for test cards, the same page on your staging system, with the same placeholders. A test pass's rescue card (your test key, or a booking forwarded to your test address) then takes the customer there, so you can try the whole journey before you go live.
