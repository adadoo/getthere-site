For card issuers

API guide for card issuers

Version 1 · Draft · Written for the developers who build it. Keys, error format, retries, test mode and webhook signatures are on the basics page. Travel site? See the travel site guide.

The short version. All you must build is one push notification of your own, after an airline charge on your card, that opens your offer. Everything else is optional. Without it, the cardholder still gets every message on WhatsApp and by email. GetThere builds the rest: the offer page, pricing, checkout, flight tracking, pre-loaded rescue cards, every message to the cardholder, and support.

1. Who builds what

You build

  • The "Just booked a flight?" push notification, from your own card data, after an airline charge. It opens your offer.
  • A push endpoint optional that shows GetThere's three messages in your app: pass bought, rescue card ready, rescue card used.
  • A purchase-match endpoint optional that answers yes or no: did this cardholder pay this airline this amount?
  • Push receipts optional: which of our pushes reached the cardholder.
  • A webhook endpoint optional for your own systems and customer service.

GetThere provides

  • Your offer pages on https://<issuer>.getthere.now, in your colours: a full page and a short one to embed in your app.
  • Your forwarding address, quote@<issuer>.getthere.now, where cardholders send their booking email.
  • The partner API at https://api.getthere.now/partner/v1.
  • Every message to the cardholder: the price, the pass, and the rescue card on WhatsApp and by email.

2. The journey, call by call

One cardholder, Mia, from booking to rebooking.

  1. Mia→Your systems

    Mia pays an airline with your card

    Your systems see the charge. Nothing reaches GetThere: card data stays with you.

  2. Your server→GetThere

    You ask for an offer

    One call per push, so a booking forwarded from it is traced back to it.

    GET /partner/v1/offer · GetThere provides it

  3. Your app→Mia

    You send "Just booked a flight?"

    It opens the offer: the short page inside your app (merchandising_url), or the full page in a browser (offer_url).

  4. Mia→GetThere

    Mia forwards her booking email

    She copies her own address from the offer and forwards the airline's confirmation to it. We read every flight and traveller on it.

  5. GetThere→Your APIoptional

    We check the charge with you

    POST <your API>/v1/getthere/purchase-match · you build it

  6. GetThere→Mia

    Mia gets a price and buys a pass

    We email her the plans within minutes. She pays at checkout on <issuer>.getthere.now.

  7. GetThere→Your push endpoint→Miaoptional

    Pushes: pass bought, rescue card ready, rescue card used

    POST <your push URL> · you build it

  8. Your systems→GetThereoptional

    You report which pushes arrived

    POST /partner/v1/push-receipts · GetThere provides it

3. How much to build

Smallest

Push and link

Your push opens https://<issuer>.getthere.now/offer. No API call at all.

  • Live in days
  • Every visit is counted, but not per push
Recommended

Traced offers in your app

Call GET /offer per push and open its merchandising_url in your app.

  • One API call
  • Each push traced to the passes it sells
  • The cardholder never leaves your app
Fullest

Everything in your app

Add your push endpoint, push receipts, purchase match and webhooks.

  • GetThere's alerts appear in your app
  • More bookings accepted at a high fare
  • Your agents see every pass event

4. GET /partner/v1/offer

You call GetThere · from your server, once per push or banner you show

Makes a fresh offer: a code, carried in the offer's links and in the forwarding address, that ties a booking forwarded from it back to this push. You'll see how many cardholders opened it, forwarded a booking, got a price and bought a pass.

GET /partner/v1/offer?utm_source=app&utm_medium=push&utm_campaign=just-booked
200 {"offer_id": "k7f2q9xm",
     "offer_url": "https://<issuer>.getthere.now/?o=k7f2q9xm",
     "merchandising_url": "https://<issuer>.getthere.now/offer/k7f2q9xm",
     "forward_to": "quote+k7f2q9xm@<issuer>.getthere.now",
     "product_name": "Flight Delay Pass",
     "reference": "R-K7F2Q9XM"}
ParameterWhat it isWhy it matters
utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign optional Where you show the offer. Up to 64 letters, digits, ., - or _, lowercased. src is short for utm_source.Your offer report splits by them, so you see which pushes, banners and campaigns sell passes.
referrer_url optionalOn a web page, the address the visitor came from.Counted by site.
opened optionaltrue when you show forward_to on your own screen at once instead of opening one of our pages.Counts the offer as opened now, so your funnel doesn't show it as never seen.
FieldWhat it isWhy it matters
offer_idThe offer's code.Store it with the push you sent, to join our numbers to yours.
merchandising_urlThe short offer page, made to sit inside your app (a web view or an iframe), in your widget settings.The cardholder stays in your app: they read the offer, copy their address and go to their email. Use it as your push's destination.
offer_urlYour full GetThere home page, with how it works and FAQs, carrying this offer's own address. /offer on your domain is the short page for your app.For a browser, an email or a banner, where there's room to explain more.
forward_toThe cardholder's forwarding address for this offer, quote+<code>@<issuer>.getthere.now. While your partner is in test mode, that address makes test passes. Once you're live, a test key gets test+<code>@<issuer>.getthere.now instead, so test bookings still make test passes.Only this full address carries the code. If you show it on your own screen, follow the rules below.
product_nameThe product's name, to show.Use it as is.
referenceWhat our pages show the cardholder as "Your reference".Quote it to us and we can find the cardholder's journey.

Errors: 401, 403 and 503 while you're paused (see errors). If the call fails, link to https://<issuer>.getthere.now/offer instead: it still works, just without tracing to the push.

5. Showing the offer in your app

<!-- In a web view, open merchandising_url full screen. Or in a page: -->
<iframe src="https://<issuer>.getthere.now/offer/k7f2q9xm" title="Flight Delay Pass"
        allow="clipboard-write"
        style="width:100%;max-width:560px;border:0;height:640px"></iframe>

Keep allow="clipboard-write": without it, browsers block the copy button inside a frame.

The page shows the offer, the cardholder's address with a copy button, and three steps. It needs no key. Opening it counts the offer as opened. The cardholder copies the address, switches to their email, and forwards their booking.

The address shown can't be copied on its own. The page shows the plain quote@<issuer>.getthere.now, which is easier to read, but only the copy button (or a tap on the address) copies the full forward_to with its code. If you show the address on your own screen instead, do the same:
  • show the address without its +code;
  • make it unselectable, with no long-press menu and no automatic mail link (on the web: user-select: none; -webkit-touch-callout: none and <meta name="format-detection" content="email=no">);
  • copy the whole forward_to when the address or a Copy button is tapped;
  • call GET /offer with opened=true.
A booking forwarded to the plain address still gets a price, but it isn't traced to your push, so your numbers undercount what your pushes sell.

Don't open a mail app with the address pre-filled. A cardholder can't forward an existing email from a new message, so it only gets in the way.

6. Your push endpoint optional

GetThere calls you · when a pass is bought, a rescue card is ready, and a rescue card is used

Without it, we send no pushes, and the cardholder still gets everything on WhatsApp and by email. With it, your app alerts them too. Pushes are alerts only: the rescue card itself always goes on WhatsApp and by email.

POST <your push URL>
Authorization: Bearer <the token you gave us>
Idempotency-Key: 5b1f…

{"push_id": "5b1f…",
 "customer": {"email": "mia@example.com"},
 "type": "card_issued",
 "title": "Your flight is 3h 50m late. Your pre-loaded rescue card with A$2,325 is ready.",
 "body": "We've sent it to you on WhatsApp and by email.",
 "link": null}
FieldWhat it isWhy it matters
push_idUnique per push, and also the Idempotency-Key header.We may retry. Show a push id only once, and report on it in push receipts.
customer.emailThe email the cardholder forwarded their booking from.How you find the cardholder. If you can't match it, answer 202 and report not_delivered.
typepass_purchased, card_issued or card_used.card_issued is time-critical: the cardholder may be at the airport. Send it at the highest priority your app has.
title, bodyReady to show, in the wording agreed with you.Show them unchanged. The wording is agreed with the Underwriter.
linkA page to open, or null.Where tapping the push should go.
202 {"accepted": true, "reference": "<your id for the push>"}

Answer as soon as you've accepted it. Report delivery later with push receipts. Give us a separate test URL and token if you have a test system. Otherwise test pushes are only recorded.

7. POST /partner/v1/push-receipts optional

You call GetThere · in batches, whenever you have them

{"receipts": [
  {"push_id": "5b1f…", "status": "delivered", "at": "2026-11-20T07:41:05+00:00"},
  {"push_id": "9c2e…", "status": "opted_out"}
]}
FieldWhat it isWhy it matters
push_id requiredFrom the push we sent.Ties the receipt to the push.
status requireddelivered, not_delivered or opted_out.opted_out stops every further push to that cardholder, so you honour their choice. not_delivered tells us WhatsApp and email are the only way to reach them.
at optionalWhen, ISO with a time zone (Z or an offset). Defaults to when we receive it. A time we can't read gives unknown_push.Shows how quickly the rescue card alert reached them.
200 {"results": [{"push_id": "5b1f…", "result": "ok"}, {"push_id": "9c2e…", "result": "ok"}]}

Each result is ok, bad_status or unknown_push (not yours, or the other mode). A body without a receipts list gets 422.

8. Your purchase-match endpoint optional

GetThere calls you · when a cardholder forwards a booking that shows its airfare

A yes confirms the booking is real and its fare is right, so we can offer a pass even when the fare looks unusually high for the route. You only answer yes or no: no transaction data comes back to us.

POST <your API>/v1/getthere/purchase-match
Authorization: Bearer <the token you gave us>

{"email": "mia@example.com", "airline": "QF", "seller": null, "amount": "1550.00", "currency": "AUD",
 "booked_after": "2025-11-20T07:41:05.120345+00:00"}
200 {"match": true}
FieldWhat it isWhy it matters
emailThe email the booking was forwarded from.How you find the cardholder. Not found is false.
airlineThe airline whose flights were bought, 2-letter code.Match on the merchant when seller is null.
sellerWho charged the card when it wasn't the airline, such as a travel agency ("Trip.com"), or null.Then the charge to look for is from the seller, not the airline.
amount, currencyThe fare on the booking.Answer true within 1% of it, so currency rounding doesn't cause a no.
booked_afterThe earliest charge to look at: a year before we ask.Keeps the search to recent charges.

We wait up to 5 seconds. A timeout or error never blocks the cardholder: the booking goes through our other checks.

9. Webhooks optional

Give us a test and a live address, and we'll tell your systems about every pass your cardholders buy: issued, ended, an email that bounced, a rescue card issued and used. The format, retries, every event and how to check the signature are on the basics page. They're most useful for your customer service: when a cardholder calls, your agent sees what we sent and when.

11. Go-live checklist

  • Your push wording, and our push wording if you use your push endpoint, is agreed with us and the Underwriter.
  • The test cases above pass.
  • If you show the address yourself, it follows the rules.
  • Your live push URL, purchase-match URL and token are with us, if you use them.
  • Your live webhook address is set and checks signatures, if you use one.
  • We issue your live key and switch you live. Swap the key: the code stays the same.
